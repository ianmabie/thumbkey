# Original mouse models

The native SceneKit models are original generated geometry, built from visual reference on October 18, 2026. They are illustrations with approximate proportions, not manufacturer CAD or dimensional scans. No reference photographs, downloaded textures, or raw scanned meshes are bundled. The geometry, studio environment, contact shadow, and small typographic surface detail are generated locally and run entirely offline.

The user also supplied Polycam USDZ scans of both mice. Both were inspected locally from multiple angles. They include desk geometry and reconstruction distortion, especially in the dark surfaces; the Naga scan's scale is not reliable. They were used as additional silhouette/proportion references rather than shipping the raw textured desk captures. A fitted desk plane in the MX scan supports a roughly 52 mm crown and a forward crown position; the illustration was adjusted accordingly. The raw scans, desk textures, and analysis artifacts remain outside the repository.

## Construction and interaction

`MouseGeometry.swift` constructs asymmetric shell meshes with monotone cubic interpolation over the physical spacing of their sections. Normals follow the nose deformation through its inverse-transpose Jacobian. Concave click-paddle contours are ear-clipped, subdivided, and projected onto the shell; their seam lines are sampled along every edge before projection so that they follow the surface instead of cutting through it. The removable Naga panel follows the shell. All three plates have individually oriented beveled caps, with textured grips on the six-/two-button plates. MX has a concave flared thumb rest with sweeping ribs, recessed wheel housings, and a surface-projected wordmark. Wheels use a single grooved, beveled lathe mesh with smooth end caps and continuous Naga light rings. Physically based materials, generated studio illumination, and a soft contact shadow replace the previous point-lit appearance. The follow-up realism pass rounds the front faces and crown cross-sections, lowers protruding control caps, blends the MX thumb ledge into the side wall, and gives the plastic subtle generated normal-map grain. Softbox reflections distinguish the matte shells from the metal wheels. Geometry is shared between the header cards and larger mapping view; both use the selected Naga plate. Cards use cached snapshots of the same scenes, explicitly displayed in original color, while the mapping view remains interactive. Selection uses restrained teal shading without alpha-dependent emission.

`SidePlate.visualRows` is the shared physical layout for the geometry and accessible button grid. In thumb-side view (nose left), the 12-button top row is 3/6/9/12, middle 2/5/8/11, bottom 1/4/7/10. The six-button rows are 1/2/3 then 6/5/4. On the two-button plate, Forward is ahead of Back. Saved configuration IDs and input identities are preserved. Physical plate detection still requires selecting the attached plate in the UI; the model does not claim to detect a hardware swap.

The Naga's five extra controls use separate shell-conforming cap meshes with tapered outlines, low bevels, and constant-width recesses. They retain individual control origins for hit testing. The MX thumb ledge is a continuous rolled profile with an underside; its extension and thickness taper into the body at both ends. The heel and thin skate surfaces follow the base instead of leaving detached rectangular edges.

The build 15 pass extends the conforming-cap construction to either flank, densely samples cap boundaries before projection, and fits the Naga ring-finger paddle to the side. The MX thumb-wheel axle follows the housing slope, Back/Forward and wheel-mode controls sit close to the body, and the thumb-rest flutes are shallow surface geometry instead of overlaid lines. Both charging ports have rounded USB-C apertures. Shared projected vertices and cached shell sections reduce construction cost while retaining precise surface normals.

Each mapped control has a stable `button-<configuration id>` scene node. Clicking the nearest visible surface selects that button; the picker does not select a hidden control through the shell. The matching button list remains available for keyboard/accessibility use. Selecting a plate changes both its mesh and its physical arrangement in the list without changing stored button identities. The view presets reset camera position; dragging and scrolling allow further inspection.

For an offline visual regression pass, run the built executable with `--render-mice <output-directory>`. This renders all four mouse/plate configurations from four preset angles plus focused front-right Naga and rear-thumb MX views (18 images), verifies the expected control IDs, and writes `model-validation.json` with ray-tested visible hit targets and exits with an error if any mapped control has no visible target, without starting input capture, opening a configuration, or changing mappings. It requires a local Metal-capable Mac. Render outputs are review artifacts and are not model textures.
